| In many network science applications, we're given a network and we are asked to find the  |
| most important or central nodes or raises according to certain criteria. For example, we  |
| may be given a communication network formed by all interactions between a group of  |
| terrorists. And we asked to identify the leader of the group, or the group member that  |
| probably knows the most information about the group's actions. Different importance  |
| criteria lead to different centrality metrics, such as closeness centrality or  |
| betweenness centrality. The objective of this lesson is to introduce you to the most  |
| common centrality metrics in the literature and explain what notion of node importance  |
| each of them tries to capture. A related problem is to identify the most important group  |
| of nodes in a network. For example, in the case of the terrorist network, the objective  |
| may be to identify the inner circle of the group. This relates to different mathematical  |
| notions of core periphery structure, and we will review the most commonly used sites  |
| approaches, such as the cake or the composition method, or the reads club notion. We will  |

## Topics Discussed

- **What makes a good test**: Validity (measures what it is supposed to measure), reliability (produces consistent results), discriminability (distinguishes levels of understanding), fairness and accessibility. Context-specific criteria such as coverage type and test plan design emerge later in the course.
- **Group assignment formation timeline**: Teams revealed one to two days before GPD0 opens; GPD0 requires only contact information. Delayed formation minimizes disruption from late drops.
- **Code reuse from prior semesters**: Policy is stated in the syllabus; students must read the syllabus before asking, as a syllabus quiz is forthcoming.
- **Gradescope sanity check**: A passing sanity check means the submission is gradeable. Plagiarism and AI-assisted code are the only additional manual review triggers. If Gradescope is satisfied and no violations occur, the grade stands.
- **AI-generated boilerplate**: IntelliJ skeleton/boilerplate code is not considered AI-generated and is acceptable. AI-assisted coding of project-specific logic is not permitted.

## Q&A

- **Q:** What makes a good test? → **A:** A good test is valid (measures what it intends to measure) and reliable (yields consistent results). It should discriminate meaningfully between levels of understanding and match the level of reasoning required. The analogy holds to academic testing — a good test reveals who understood the material.
- **Q:** When will group assignments be known? → **A:** One to two days before GPD0 opens. GPD0 is only contact information; do not stress about it.
- **Q:** Can a student who took the course previously reuse their own code? → **A:** See the syllabus.
- **Q:** Does IntelliJ skeleton code count as AI-generated? → **A:** No. Boilerplate generated by the IDE predates LLMs and is not considered AI-generated.
- **Q:** How should students prepare for future assignments? → **A:** Watch lectures, read assignment instructions carefully, follow Gradescope feedback. Spend more time planning than implementing — a rushed solution that requires complete rework costs far more time.

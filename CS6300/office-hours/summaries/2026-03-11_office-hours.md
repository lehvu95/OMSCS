## Topics Discussed

- **A6 communication lockdown policy:** During the A6 submission week, public discussion posts are suspended. Private posts remain permitted. The instructional team may selectively make certain private Q&A public if broadly applicable, with student consent. Students should continue checking the discussion board, as clarification posts may be published.
- **A6 Gradescope sanity check:** Students must pass the Gradescope sanity check before submission. Sanity check gives a maximum of 10/100; a score of 0/10 indicates a structural error that will result in zero points on the final grade regardless of content correctness. Ignoring a 0/10 result will not be grounds for a grade appeal.
- **A6 learning objectives:** A6 exercises understanding of white-box coverage criteria (statement, branch, MCDC, path), their relationships via subsumption, and the practical tradeoffs between thoroughness and feasibility. No criterion provides a guarantee of correctness; each covers a different subset of program behavior. The assignment rewards understanding the mathematical relationships between criteria more than the volume of writing.
- **Property-based testing:** Paula discussed property-based testing as an intermediate approach between traditional testing and formal verification. Assessed as less approachable than unit testing and less rigorous than formal verification; useful in specific contexts (e.g., verifying operation composition) but not a general substitute for either approach.
- **Formal verification and AI:** Extended discussion on whether AI can lower the barrier to applying formal verification. Consensus: AI may assist in making formal methods more accessible, but cannot substitute for human expertise, particularly in safety-critical contexts where even 80% automated correctness is insufficient.
- **Coverage criteria and black-box vs. white-box testing as a path toward formal methods:** Paula argued that black-box testing, rooted in requirements, may serve as a better conceptual bridge to formal specification than white-box testing, which is code-focused. The key transition is learning to reason about invariants and properties rather than individual test inputs.
